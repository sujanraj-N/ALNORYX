Designed around customer boundaries

A database model that can grow with you.

Every customer's projects and tasks should be tied to a workspace, with permissions checked against membership and role.

TablePurposeCore rule
profilesUser profile and preferencesUsers can only change permitted profile fields
workspacesCustomer team or accountAccess limited to authorized members
workspace_membersMembership and rolesRole changes restricted to authorized owners/admins
projectsProjects in a workspaceWorkspace membership required
tasksAssignments, status, deadlinesProject and workspace access verified
commentsTask and project discussionsOnly permitted members can read or post
attachmentsPrivate file metadataPrivate bucket and authorized downloads
subscriptionsBilling state and provider IDsPaid access based on verified server-side state
audit_eventsSecurity and admin activityRestricted access; never store secrets
Least privilege

Default-deny access

Enable row-level security on exposed tables, grant only required permissions, and test allowed and denied read/write actions with separate accounts.

Private files

Access-controlled uploads

Use private storage, validate size and file type, use safe paths, and check authorization before generating download access.

Reliable records

Backups and audit trails

Monitor important events without logging passwords or tokens. Regularly test that backups can actually be restored.

From prototype to product

A realistic route to your first customers.

Build evidence before investing heavily in growth. The goal is a product that solves a repeat problem, not a long feature list.

Step 01

Talk to 5–10 people

Interview freelancers or small agencies. Learn how they track projects today, where work gets delayed, and what tools they already pay for.

Step 02

Ship one full workflow

Create a project, assign a task, set a deadline, update progress, and view a report. Make that flow dependable before adding extras.

Step 03

Run a private beta

Invite a small group, observe friction, fix common problems, and provide a clear way to ask for help.

Step 04

Offer a transparent pilot

State what's included, what is still being built, how support works, and how a customer can cancel.

Step 05

Measure real value

Track weekly active teams, completed projects, repeat use, support requests, conversions, and cancellations.

Step 06

Grow when evidence supports it

Prioritize features that help customers return. Avoid large advertising spend until you understand why people choose the product.

Illustrative arithmetic, not a forecast

Understand gross revenue before expenses

At ₹399 per month, 25 paying customers would represent ₹9,975 in gross monthly revenue; 100 would represent ₹39,900. These are examples only, not guaranteed earnings, and exclude hosting, payment fees, taxes, refunds, support, and customer acquisition.

Plan carefully

Know the costs before launch.

Start with a small workload and check current provider limits and terms. Free tiers can change and may not cover production needs.

Core infrastructure

Domain renewal, app hosting, database/authentication, private file storage, backups, and monitoring.

Customer operations

Verification and notification email, support time, documentation, and incident response.

Business expenses

Payment and billing fees, refunds, disputes, applicable taxes, legal/admin obligations, and optional AI usage.

An early prototype might fit a ₹0–₹3,000/month infrastructure planning allowance, but that is only a rough estimate—not a price promise—and may exclude a domain, taxes, paid tools, professional review, and transaction fees. Check current payment pricing and local eligibility before choosing a provider. Since business accounts and contracts can have age requirements, work with a trusted adult when needed.
